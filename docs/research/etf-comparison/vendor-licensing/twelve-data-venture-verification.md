## CRITICAL CORRECTIONS TO PRIOR RESEARCH

**1. "Venture at $499/month is the lowest published tier granting External display data access" — WRONG on price, right on tier.**

Venture is a **tier with three published price points**, and *all three* carry the same feature set including external display. Twelve Data's own pricing sheet states:

> "| Venture | $149/mo | 610 | 500 | 70+ | External display data, real-time US/EU |
> | Venture | $299/mo | 1,597 | 1,500 | 70+ | More throughput on the same Venture feature set |
> | Venture | $499/mo | 2,584 | 2,500 | 70+ | Top of Venture tier |"

— [twelvedata.com/pricing.md](https://twelvedata.com/pricing.md)

The business pricing comparison table on the human-readable page confirms the entry price: **"Venture From $149 /mo"** ([twelvedata.com/pricing-business](https://twelvedata.com/pricing-business)). The $499 headline on the card is the *top* Venture configuration (2,584 API + 2,500 WS credits) that happens to be pre-selected in the credits dropdown.

**The lowest published price with "External display data access" is therefore $149/month, not $499/month.** $499 buys throughput, not rights.

**2. ETF reference data (expense ratio, net assets, NAV, inception date, holdings/composition) is NOT included in Venture. It requires Enterprise ($1,099/mo).** This is the single biggest gap for Outfox's stated product. See Q3/Q4 and the "Specific factual claims" section below. Every `/etfs/world/*` endpoint doc carries the identical note:

> "This API endpoint is available on the Ultra plan (individual) and the Enterprise plan (business) and above."

— [/etfs/world/summary](https://twelvedata.com/docs/llms/etfs/etf-summary.md), [/etfs/world/composition](https://twelvedata.com/docs/llms/etfs/etf-composition.md), [/etfs/world/performance](https://twelvedata.com/docs/llms/etfs/etf-performance.md), [/etfs/world/risk](https://twelvedata.com/docs/llms/etfs/etf-risk.md), [/etfs/world](https://twelvedata.com/docs/llms/etfs/etf-all-data.md)

Consistently, the business pricing page lists **"ETFs metrics data"** as a feature of **Enterprise** ("Everything from Venture, plus … ETFs metrics data"), not of Venture ([twelvedata.com/pricing-business](https://twelvedata.com/pricing-business)).

**3. There is an explicit anti-competition clause.** Terms §2.3(d) and §2.3(k). This is the most consequential finding in the document. See Q13 and the dedicated section.

### Is there a clause prohibiting building a competing or similar service?

**YES. THERE IS. This is the most important finding in this report, and the prior research's implicit assumption that Twelve Data is cleaner than Tiingo here is wrong.**

Twelve Data has **two** anti-competition clauses, both in Terms §2.3 "Restrictions," which opens **"Customer shall not, and shall ensure its Authorized Users do not:"**:

> **"(d) Use the Platform to build competitive products or services"**

> **"(k) Combine Data with other sources to create competing products without permission"**

— [twelvedata.com/terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms), §2.3

Two adjacent clauses tighten the same screw:

> **"(f) Create derivative financial products without explicit written permission"**

> **"(g) Store or cache Data beyond permitted timeframes specified in the Documentation"**

And in §17.1 Acceptable Use Policy — "Customer shall not":

> **"(g) Create derivative works for redistribution"**

**Comparison with Tiingo, on the two limbs the prior research flagged:**

| Tiingo restriction | Twelve Data equivalent? | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Barring use "to build a similar or competitive website, application or service" | **YES, and arguably broader.** §2.3(d) "Use the Platform to build competitive products or services" — Tiingo at least qualifies with "similar or competitive"; Twelve Data's §2.3(d) is a bare, undefined prohibition with **no definition of "competitive" in §1** and no carve-out. §2.3(k) extends it to combinations with third-party data. | **Twelve Data is NOT cleaner than Tiingo here. It is arguably worse drafted, because "competitive" is nowhere defined and there is no "similar" qualifier to anchor scope.** |
| Barring "publishing or otherwise making available to the public any analysis of the Service or Tiingo Data" | **NO equivalent found.** I searched the full Terms, the AUP (§17), the attribution guidelines, and all support articles read. **Why §2.3(d) is a live risk for Outfox specifically, not a boilerplate one.** Twelve Data itself publishes, on its own public website, exactly the product Outfox proposes to build: per-symbol ETF pages showing NAV, expense ratio, net assets, performance and holdings (e.g. the `twelvedata.com/markets/…` fund pages surfaced in search), plus `/etfs/world/performance` (trailing and annual total returns), `/etfs/world/risk` (`std`, `sharpe_ratio`, `beta`), and `/etfs/world/composition` (`top_holdings` with weights) as paid API products. Outfox's site would display **computed returns, computed volatility, expense ratios and holdings overlap** — i.e. the visible output of three Twelve Data paid endpoints, free and ad-supported, to the public.

A reasonable counterparty could characterise that as a "competitive product" under §2.3(d) and, if Outfox sources expense ratios or holdings elsewhere to stay on Venture, as a "combination with other sources to create competing products" under §2.3(k). Written vendor confirmation, in a signed order form or side letter naming Outfox's specific use case and expressly carving it out of §2.3(d) and §2.3(k), is required before Outfox writes code against Twelve Data.**
