# Outfox brand messaging

Status: approved brand direction  
Last reviewed: September 2, 2026

## Primary pitch

> Outfox tracks what influential investors, corporate insiders, and members of
> Congress are doing with their money. We turn those public disclosures into
> understandable patterns that help ordinary investors discover what may be
> worth investigating.

Use this as the default short description for the website, profiles, partnership
introductions, and other places where Outfox must be explained quickly.

## Supporting narrative

Outfox helps ordinary investors understand where informed money is moving.

We bring together publicly disclosed activity from influential Wall Street
investors, corporate insiders, and members of Congress. Then we identify the
companies, sectors, and themes appearing across those groups and explain what
those patterns could mean in plain English.

Investing can feel intimidating—and sometimes rigged in favor of people with
better information. Outfox pulls back the curtain, giving everyone a clearer
starting point for deciding what is worth investigating.

## Positioning line

> See where informed money is moving—and understand why it matters.

## Governing product questions

Every major feature, report, email, and social post should help answer at least
one of these questions:

1. Where is informed money moving?
2. Which groups are converging on the same companies or themes?
3. Why might the pattern matter?
4. How current and reliable is the evidence?
5. What could a reader investigate next?
6. What can the evidence not establish?

## Voice and presentation rules

- Meet readers where they are. Define financial language instead of assuming it.
- Lead with the useful finding, then show the evidence and its source.
- Clearly distinguish a reported holding, a disclosed transaction, and an interpretation.
- Use “worth investigating” rather than promising that a stock should be bought or will produce a return.
- Remain politically neutral: Outfox follows disclosed activity, not parties.
- Pair every important claim with timing, provenance, and limitations.
- Make the information visually clear enough to understand before asking the reader to study a table.
- Keep website UI, Outfox Reports, email, and social posts part of the same evidence-first story.

## Channel application

### Website

Use the positioning line as the clearest entry point. The next screen should
visually demonstrate convergence among public officials, corporate insiders,
and influential managers rather than merely describing it.

### Email and Outfox Reports

State what changed, identify who disclosed it, explain where groups agree or
disagree, and link to the underlying Outfox evidence page. Never manufacture
urgency when the source itself is delayed.

### Social posts

Lead with one verifiable observation and one useful interpretation. Link to an
Outfox page containing the sources, dates, methodology, and limitations.

### Subscription messaging

The free experience explains the landscape. Paid features should save readers
time or add personalization through alerts, watchlists, deeper comparisons, and
earlier or more detailed reports—not hide basic source verification behind a paywall.
